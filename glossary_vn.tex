\section{Style rules}

\begin{enumerate}[label=S\arabic*]
  \item \textbf{Voice and register.} ``We'' is \vi{chúng tôi}; ``the author'' (the AI statement,
        the acknowledgments) is \vi{tác giả}. Formal academic Vietnamese, in sentences as short
        as the English ones. No claim is added or dropped, and the hedges stay: mostly
        \vi{chủ yếu}, about \vi{khoảng}, at least \vi{ít nhất}, at most \vi{nhiều nhất}, often
        \vi{thường}, to our knowledge \vi{theo hiểu biết của chúng tôi}.
  \item \textbf{Terms.} Always the Vietnamese term of Section~\ref{sec:terms}. For the terms
        whose note says ``first:'', the English term follows in parentheses at the first mention
        in the abstract and again at the first in the body, as in \vi{giai đoạn khởi động
        (warm-up)}. Since 20 November, meta-path (N17) and embedding (N23) are no longer glossed:
        they stay in English.
  \item \textbf{Words with two meanings.} A product review is \vi{nhận xét} and its rating
        \vi{số sao} (N14); \vi{đánh giá} is evaluating a model. \vi{Thử nghiệm} is the test data
        and testing on it (N11), so an experiment is \vi{thực nghiệm} and a check \vi{kiểm tra};
        \vi{xác thực} is the validation data, and \vi{kiểm định} a statistical test (\vi{kiểm
        định t}). \vi{Tập} alone is a path set; the data are \vi{tập huấn luyện}, \vi{tập xác
        thực} and \vi{tập thử nghiệm}.
  \item \textbf{What stays in English} (Section~\ref{sec:english}): the names of models,
        metrics, paths and sets, code and files, datasets and venues; the reference list (N7);
        ACM's CCS terms (N9).
  \item \textbf{Numbers.} Every number is \verb|\val{key}| (N4). Design constants typed in the
        text take the same format: \vi{0,005}, \vi{80/10/10}. Units: \vi{giờ GPU}, \vi{phút GPU},
        GB, and \vi{triệu} for a million (``27.4M parameters'' becomes \vi{27,4 triệu tham số}),
        each tied to its number so that a line never starts with the unit
        (\verb|\val{...}~giờ~GPU|).
        Ranges take an en dash (57--82); \% follows the number without a space. Where the English
        writes a small number in words, so does the Vietnamese (four paths, \vi{bốn meta-path}).
  \item \textbf{Cross-references,} always with a tie: \vi{Mục}~\verb|\ref| for a section or
        subsection, \vi{Bảng}, \vi{Hình}, \vi{Phụ lục}, and \vi{công thức}~\verb|\eqref|. Several
        take the singular: \vi{Mục 6 và 7}, \vi{Bảng 9 và 10}, \vi{Hình 1b}.
  \item \textbf{Citations} as in the English (\verb|\citep|, \verb|\citet|). A place in the cited
        work is written in Vietnamese: \vi{[10, Bảng 4]}, \vi{[7, Phụ lục D]}, \vi{[14, §3.3]}.
        ``et al.''\ stays (N7).
  \item \textbf{Quotations} keep the cited paper's English words, inside quotation marks and set
        as English (\verb|\foreignlanguage{english}{...}|), followed by a Vietnamese translation
        in parentheses and then the citation (N13): ``highly sensitive to the meta-paths they
        use'' (\vi{rất nhạy với các meta-path mà chúng sử dụng}) [17].
  \item \textbf{Headings} in sentence case: only the first word and names take a capital
        (\vi{Kết quả theo nhóm sản phẩm}). \code{acmart} sets section headings in capitals by
        itself. A \verb|\paragraph| heading ends with a full stop, as in the English.
  \item \textbf{Lists and punctuation.} No comma before \vi{và}, in a list (\vi{A, B và C}) or
        between two clauses (N20). Where dropping the comma would leave a sentence ambiguous, the
        sentence is split, or \vi{và} gives way to the word the comma stood for: \vi{rồi} for a
        sequence, \vi{còn} for a contrast, \vi{nên} for a consequence, \vi{đồng thời} for two facts
        at once. No semicolons either (N20). Two clauses become two sentences, items inside
        parentheses take a comma, a list whose items contain commas is numbered (1), (2), and a
        table cell takes a comma or a full stop, never a full stop before a number, since
        thousands take a dot (15.831). No space before : ? ! or \%. Quotation marks are \vi{“ ”},
        written \verb|``...''|.
  \item \textbf{Emphasis:} \verb|\emph| marks a term where it is defined, as in the English
        (\vi{\emph{nhóm đồng hạng}}).
  \item \textbf{Line breaks.} Vietnamese text is not hyphenated. A line that overflows is fixed by
        rewording, or by \verb|\foreignlanguage{english}{...}| around a long English term.
\end{enumerate}
